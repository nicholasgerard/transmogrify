To transmogrify is to change something completely, as if by magic. The word has
been in English since the mid-seventeenth century and its origin is still
uncertain — see [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/transmogrify)
and [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/transmogrify).

Cucumbers becoming pickles is the useful image. The result is still
recognizably the thing you started with, but its form and its possibilities are
not the same, and the change does not run backwards.

That is the architecture, not just the branding. Transmogrify turns
provider-specific native mechanisms into one lifecycle **without** flattening
them into a fake common transport. Codex, Claude Code, and future desktop
integrations each keep their native channel while exposing consistent
ownership, visibility, steering, recovery, retirement, and cleanup semantics.
Codex and Claude Code are the first complete pair, not the final boundary.
